\title{Delayed-Disclosure Twirling for Additive Secret Sharing:\\
Exact Leakage Identities and Minimax Local Randomization}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study additive secret sharing in a temporal leakage experiment.  Before a
single local-leakage snapshot, one share is translated by a hidden uniform group
element and the other shares are transformed by hidden random automorphisms.  The
recovery metadata is disclosed after the snapshot.  For additive sharing over a
field of order $Q$, we prove that, for every fixed secret and arbitrary
conditionally independent local stochastic channels, the transcript has exact
$\chitwo$ divergence
\[
    \frac{\prod_{i=1}^d e_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}
\]
from the product of its recovery-metadata and local-leakage marginals, where
$e_i$ is the $\chitwo$ information of the $i$th channel under a uniform input.
The identity implies pairwise statistical secrecy and an arbitrary-prior mutual-
information bound.
We extend the identity to finite abelian groups, where the answer decomposes over
orbits of the automorphism action on the dual group.  Because real channels have
equal energy at conjugate characters, these terms combine into inversion-closed
dual orbits.  For arbitrary finite abelian groups, we prove that the smallest
such orbit is the exact minimax parameter throughout an explicit
character-order-dependent budget range, always larger than $1/2$.  We determine
the exact energy capacity of a conjugate-character packet, and packet-supported
channels give the matching lower bound for every joint distribution on the
automorphisms.  We characterize optimal actions on
elementary abelian groups and the exact projective seed requirement for field
multipliers at every strictly interior feasible budget.  A full-spectrum erasure
witness extends the minimax
value, though not the uniqueness classification, to every feasible energy budget
whenever there is a single nontrivial inversion-closed orbit.  Finally, we show that
metadata-aware leakage can cancel every local bijection, making the disclosure
order essential.  The result is one-shot and does not cover joint leakage or
remeasurement after disclosure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Local leakage of additive and linear secret sharing has been studied using
Fourier analysis, additive combinatorics, coding theory, and physical leakage
models~\cite{bdir18,mpsw21,maji22tight,maji22bounded,faust24practice}.
The basic difficulty is spectral alignment: additive sharing forces the same
nontrivial character to appear in the Fourier contribution of every local
leakage function.  An attacker can exploit a common high-energy character across
the shares.

This paper asks what can be obtained by randomizing that alignment before a
one-time leakage snapshot.  The randomizer is kept outside the leaking components
during the snapshot and is disclosed afterward so that the original secret can
still be reconstructed.  The construction is elementary: translate one share
uniformly and multiply every other field share by an independent uniform nonzero
scalar.  Its analysis gives an exact identity for arbitrary finite-output
stochastic channels, not merely an asymptotic upper bound.

The translation removes cross-character terms in the squared likelihood ratio.
The multipliers then average every nontrivial character over the same orbit.
This exposes the governing invariant: the distribution of each local channel's
nonconstant Fourier energy across dual-group orbits.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Within the temporal experiment formalized in Section~\ref{sec:model}, we prove:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item an exact, fixed-secret $\chitwo$ identity over every finite field,
          with pairwise statistical and arbitrary-prior mutual-information
          consequences;
    \item an exact dual-orbit identity for finite abelian groups and arbitrary
          automorphism subgroups;
    \item a minimax classification for arbitrary finite abelian groups showing
          that the smallest inversion-closed dual orbit is the exact security
          parameter throughout an explicit character-order-dependent local
          $\chitwo$ budget range;
    \item the exact energy capacity of a channel supported on one conjugate
          character packet;
    \item optimality of projectively transitive actions on elementary abelian
          groups, together with an exact seed-entropy characterization for field
          multipliers at every strictly interior feasible budget;
    \item a full-energy minimax identity for actions with one nontrivial real
          orbit; and
    \item an impossibility observation showing that arbitrary leakage functions
          can undo local bijections when the metadata is available during leakage.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.}
Leakage followed by a reveal phase is established in stronger secret-sharing
models~\cite{ckos21,hvw25}; those works reveal additional shares or support
equivocation, rather than analyzing the hidden encoding metadata used here.
After-the-fact leakage is also established terminology in other primitives.
Our transcript is also a designated-unauthorized-set instance of the standard
nonadaptive local-leakage experiment~\cite{sv19}.  We do not claim that the
chronology itself is new.  Our model is weaker than
adaptive or joint leakage: channels cannot depend on the hidden recovery capsule,
their outputs are conditionally independent, and no new leakage is taken after
disclosure.  Section~\ref{sec:related} gives a conservative comparison.

\section{Preliminaries}

For distributions $P$ and $M$ on a finite set with $P\ll M$, define
\[
  \chitwo(P\Vert M)
    = \sum_{\omega}M(\omega)
      \left(\frac{P(\omega)}{M(\omega)}-1\right)^2
\]
and
\[
  \TV(P,M)=\frac12\sum_{\omega}|P(\omega)-M(\omega)|.
\]
We write
\[
  D(P\Vert M)=\sum_\omega P(\omega)
       \log\frac{P(\omega)}{M(\omega)}
\]
for relative entropy, with natural logarithms.  Information quantities are
therefore measured in nats; division by $\log 2$ gives bits.
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tvchi}
  \TV(P,M)\leq \frac12\sqrt{\chitwo(P\Vert M)}.
\end{equation}

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, written additively.  Its dual $\widehat G$
is the group of homomorphisms $\gamma:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}$ whose images have
unit modulus.  We use normalized expectation for Fourier coefficients.  Thus,
for $f:G\to\mathbb{C}$,
\[
    \widehat f(\gamma)=\E_{x\leftarrow G}\gamma(x)f(x).
\]
Using a conjugate in this definition only relabels characters.  Orthogonality and
Parseval take the forms
\begin{align}
 \E_x\gamma(x)\overline{\psi(x)}&=\mathbf 1[\gamma=\psi],\label{eq:orth}\\
 \E_x|f(x)|^2&=\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat G}|\widehat f(\gamma)|^2.
 \label{eq:parseval}
\end{align}

\subsection{Local channel energy}

Let $W:G\to\mathcal Z$ be a finite-output stochastic channel and let $X$ be
uniform in $G$.  Put
\[
  q(z)=\E_X W(z\mid X),\qquad
  g(x,z)=\frac{W(z\mid x)}{q(z)}-1
\]
for $z$ with $q(z)>0$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 e(W)=\chitwo(P_{X,Z}\Vert P_XP_Z)
     =\E_{x\leftarrow G,z\leftarrow q}|g(x,z)|^2.
\end{equation}
Since $\E_xg(x,z)=0$, Parseval implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-energy}
 e(W)=\sum_{\gamma\neq 1}E_W(\gamma),\qquad
 E_W(\gamma)=\E_{z\leftarrow q}
     \left|\E_{x\leftarrow G}\gamma(x)g(x,z)\right|^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Output-alphabet bound]\label{lem:alphabet}
For every finite-output channel $W:G\to\mathcal Z$,
\[
 e(W)\leq \min\{|G|-1,|\supp(Z)|-1\}.
\]
If $W$ is a deterministic function with exactly $r$ attained outputs, then
$e(W)=r-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Form the normalized joint matrix
\[
 K_{x,z}=\frac{P_{X,Z}(x,z)}{\sqrt{P_X(x)P_Z(z)}}.
\]
It represents a conditional-expectation operator, so its singular values are at
most one.  The constant functions give one singular value equal to one, while
\[
 \|K\|_{\mathrm F}^2
   =\sum_{x,z}\frac{P_{X,Z}(x,z)^2}{P_X(x)P_Z(z)}
   =1+e(W).
\]
Since $\operatorname{rank}K\leq\min\{|G|,|\supp(Z)|\}$, the other squared
singular values sum to at most the stated minimum.  For deterministic $W$ with $r$ nonempty
fibers, the $r$ normalized columns are orthonormal, so all $r$ nonzero singular
values equal one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Full-spectrum erasure channel]\label{lem:erasure}
Let $|G|=Q$.  For every $0\leq\varepsilon\leq Q-1$, there is a channel
$W_\varepsilon$ with $e(W_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon$ and
\[
 E_{W_\varepsilon}(\gamma)=\frac{\varepsilon}{Q-1}
 \qquad(\gamma\neq1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\rho=\varepsilon/(Q-1)$ and use output alphabet $G\cup\{\bot\}$, with
\[
 W_\varepsilon(\bot\mid x)=1-\rho,\qquad
 W_\varepsilon(y\mid x)=\rho\mathbf1[y=x].
\]
The erasure output has zero centered response.  For $y\in G$ and
$\gamma\neq1$, its centered reveal response has Fourier coefficient
\[
 \E_x\gamma(x)(Q\mathbf1[x=y]-1)=\gamma(y).
\]
Averaging its squared magnitude with output mass $\rho/Q$ over all $y$ gives
$E_{W_\varepsilon}(\gamma)=\rho$.  Summing the $Q-1$ nontrivial frequencies
gives the claimed total energy.
\end{proof}

\section{Delayed-disclosure experiment}\label{sec:model}

Let $F=\F_Q$ and fix $d\geq2$.  To share $s\in F$, sample
$U_1,\ldots,U_{d-1}$ independently and uniformly, and set
\[
 U_d=s-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}U_i.
\]
Independently sample
\[
 B\leftarrow F,\qquad A_2,\ldots,A_d\leftarrow F^{*}
\]
uniformly, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:encoding}
 X_1=U_1+B,qquad X_i=A_iU_i\quad(2\leq i\leq d).
\end{equation}
The recovery capsule is $R=(B,A_2,\ldots,A_d)$, and correctness follows from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reconstruct}
 s=(X_1-B)+\sum_{i=2}^{d}A_i^{-1}X_i.
\end{equation}

Before $R$ is sampled, an adversary fixes finite-output local channels
$W_i:F\to\mathcal Z_i$.  A single snapshot samples the $Z_i$ independently
conditional on the stored values, with $Z_i\sim W_i(\cdot\mid X_i)$.  The
capsule $R$ is then disclosed.  The adversary may choose the channels after
choosing two challenge secrets, but cannot make the channels depend on $R$ or
take another snapshot after disclosure.

For each $i$, let $q_i$ and $e_i=e(W_i)$ be defined as in
Equations~\eqref{eq:energy}--\eqref{eq:spectral-energy} for a uniform field
input.  Every $X_i$ is marginally uniform for every fixed secret.  Consequently,
$q_i$ is also the fixed-secret marginal of $Z_i$.

\subsection{Relation to the standard local-leakage game}

The temporal presentation is useful operationally, but it does not define a new
indistinguishability game.  Treat the capsule as one additional, designated
component.

\begin{proposition}[Standard-game embedding]\label{prop:embedding}
The tuple $(R,X_1,\ldots,X_d)$ is a perfect $(d+1)$-out-of-$(d+1)$ secret
sharing of $s$, with a nonuniform share size.  In the nonadaptive local-leakage
game, fix the unauthorized set to the capsule component, reveal $R$ completely,
and apply capsule-independent local channels $W_i$ to the remaining components.
The resulting view is identically distributed to $(R,Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)$ in the
delayed-disclosure experiment.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Correctness is Equation~\eqref{eq:reconstruct}.  If $R$ is present but some
$X_j$ is absent, invert the known local bijections.  The view then contains at
most $d-1$ additive shares, whose distribution is independent of $s$.  If $R$
is absent, the entire tuple $(X_1,\ldots,X_d)$ is independent and uniform:
$B$ makes $X_1$ uniform independently of $(U_2,\ldots,U_d)$, that latter tuple
is uniform for fixed $s$, and the independently sampled nonzero multipliers
preserve uniformity.  Thus every proper component set has perfect privacy.

In the stated nonadaptive game, the channels are fixed without the corrupted
component $R$.  Sampling the sharing, evaluating the local channels, and then
returning $R$ gives exactly the joint law in our experiment; changing only the
order in which the already sampled outputs are handed to the adversary does not
change that law.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:embedding} is only an embedding for the designated
unauthorized set consisting of the capsule.  We do not prove local-leakage
resilience for every unauthorized set, as required of a full leakage-resilient
secret-sharing construction.  Moreover, allowing the leakage channels to depend
on the revealed component is the stronger local-leakage order and is impossible
for these bijections by Proposition~\ref{prop:cancellation} below.

\section{Exact field identity}

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-secret identity]\label{thm:field}
For every $s\in F$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field-main}
 \chitwo\left(
    P_{R,Z_1,\ldots,Z_d\mid S=s}
    \middle\Vert
    P_R\prod_{i=1}^{d}q_i
 \right)
 =\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{d}e_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the value is independent of $s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $z_i$, write
\[
 g_i(x,z_i)=\frac{W_i(z_i\mid x)}{q_i(z_i)}-1,
 \qquad
 c_i(\gamma,z_i)=\E_{x\leftarrow F}\gamma(x)g_i(x,z_i).
\]
The trivial coefficient is zero.  Relative to $d$ independent uniform field
elements, the density of additive shares of $s$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint}
 Q\mathbf 1\left[\sum_i u_i=s\right]
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat F}\gamma\left(\sum_i u_i-s\right).
\end{equation}

Fix $r=(b,a_2,\ldots,a_d)$ and let
\[
 \Lambda_{s,r}(z)=
 \frac{P[Z=z\mid S=s,R=r]}{\prod_i q_i(z_i)}.
\]
Substitute $u_1=x_1-b$ and $u_i=a_i^{-1}x_i$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:constraint}.  Expanding
$\prod_i(1+g_i(x_i,z_i))$, every nontrivial-character term containing a
constant local factor has zero expectation.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood-expansion}
 \Lambda_{s,r}(z)-1
 =\sum_{\gamma\neq1}
   \gamma(-s-b)c_1(\gamma,z_1)
   \prod_{i=2}^{d}c_i(\gamma\circ m_{a_i^{-1}},z_i),
\end{equation}
where $m_a(x)=ax$.

Square Equation~\eqref{eq:likelihood-expansion} and average with respect to
$P_R\prod_iq_i$.  By orthogonality,
\[
 \E_B\gamma(-B)\overline{\psi(-B)}=\mathbf1[\gamma=\psi].
\]
All cross-character terms therefore vanish.  The secret-dependent phase has
modulus one.  For every fixed nontrivial $\gamma$, uniform $A_i\in F^{*}$
makes $\gamma\circ m_{A_i^{-1}}$ uniform among all $Q-1$ nontrivial additive
characters.  Thus
\[
 \E_{A_i,Z_i}
   |c_i(\gamma\circ m_{A_i^{-1}},Z_i)|^2
 =\frac{e_i}{Q-1}.
\]
The local outputs and multipliers are independent, so the remaining diagonal
sum is
\[
 \sum_{\gamma\neq1}
   \E_{Z_1}|c_1(\gamma,Z_1)|^2
   \prod_{i=2}^{d}\frac{e_i}{Q-1}
 =\frac{\prod_i e_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}},
\]
using Equation~\eqref{eq:spectral-energy} for the first coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pairwise statistical secrecy]\label{cor:tv}
For all $s,t\in F$,
\[
 \TV(P_{R,Z\mid S=s},P_{R,Z\mid S=t})
 \leq
 \sqrt{\frac{\prod_i e_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}}.
\]
If each leakage output has at most $2^{\ell_i}$ values, then the right-hand side
is at most
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{\prod_i(2^{\ell_i}-1)}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use Equation~\eqref{eq:tvchi}, the triangle inequality through the common
reference $P_R\prod_iq_i$, Theorem~\ref{thm:field}, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:alphabet}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Arbitrary-prior information leakage]\label{cor:mutual-information}
Let $S$ have any distribution on $F$, independently of the sharing and capsule
randomness, and put $T=(R,Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)$.  Then
\[
 I(S;T)\leq
 \log\left(1+\frac{\prod_i e_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}\right).
\]
If each leakage output has at most $2^{\ell_i}$ values, the numerator may be
replaced by $\prod_i(2^{\ell_i}-1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $P_s=P_{T\mid S=s}$, $P_T=\sum_s\Prb[S=s]P_s$, and
$M=P_R\prod_iq_i$.  The relative-entropy barycenter identity gives
\[
 \sum_s\Prb[S=s]D(P_s\Vert M)
   =I(S;T)+D(P_T\Vert M).
\]
For any $P\ll M$, Jensen's inequality under $P$ gives
\[
 D(P\Vert M)
  \leq \log\E_{\omega\leftarrow P}\frac{P(\omega)}{M(\omega)}
  =\log\bigl(1+\chitwo(P\Vert M)\bigr).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:field} gives the same value of the chi-square divergence for
every $s$.  Dropping the nonnegative term $D(P_T\Vert M)$ proves the first
claim; Lemma~\ref{lem:alphabet} proves the alphabet-size specialization.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Equal-alphabet scaling]\label{rem:scaling}
If every leakage output has support size at most $L$, put
$\rho=(L-1)/(Q-1)$.  Corollaries~\ref{cor:tv} and
\ref{cor:mutual-information} give
\[
 \TV(P_{T\mid S=s},P_{T\mid S=t})
    \leq \sqrt{Q-1}\,\rho^{d/2},
 \qquad
 I(S;T)\leq\log\bigl(1+(Q-1)\rho^d\bigr).
\]
Thus, when $1<L<Q$, pairwise distance is at most a target $\delta>0$ whenever
\[
 d\geq
 \frac{\log(Q-1)-2\log\delta}
      {\log((Q-1)/(L-1))}.
\]
For $L=1$, all local outputs are constant and both leakage measures vanish.
The formula gives an upper bound, not a claim that bounded-alphabet channels
attain it simultaneously for every parameter choice.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite abelian groups and dual orbits}

Let $G$ be a finite abelian group and $H\leq\Aut(G)$.  Additively share
$s\in G$, translate the first share by uniform $B\in G$, and apply independent
uniform $h_i\in H$ to shares $2,\ldots,d$.  Reconstruction applies the inverse
automorphisms after subtracting $B$.

The group $H$ acts on $\widehat G$ by
\[
 h\cdot\gamma=\gamma\circ h^{-1}.
\]
For a nontrivial orbit $\mathcal O\subseteq\widehat G$, define
\[
 e_i(\mathcal O)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal O}E_{W_i}(\gamma).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact orbit identity]\label{thm:orbit}
In the preceding experiment, for every fixed $s\in G$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit}
 \chitwo\left(P_{R,Z\mid S=s}\middle\Vert P_R\prod_iq_i\right)
 =\sum_{\substack{\mathcal O\text{ an }H\text{-orbit}\\
                   \mathcal O\neq\{1\}}}
   \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{d}e_i(\mathcal O)}
        {|\mathcal O|^{d-1}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The translation average removes cross-character terms exactly as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:field}.  By orbit--stabilizer, a uniform $h\in H$ maps any
fixed $\gamma$ uniformly over its orbit.  Thus, for $i\geq2$, the averaged local
energy at $\gamma$ is $e_i(\mathcal O_\gamma)/|\mathcal O_\gamma|$.
Summing the first local energy over $\gamma$ and grouping by orbit gives
Equation~\eqref{eq:orbit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Field multiplication is transitive on the nontrivial additive characters of
$\F_Q$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:field} is therefore the single-nontrivial-orbit case
of Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit}.
\end{remark}

\section{Real dual orbits and minimax automorphism randomization}

Complex characters occur in conjugate pairs because channel probabilities are
real.  Indeed, $g_i(x,z)$ is real, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjugate-energy}
 E_{W_i}(\gamma^{-1})=E_{W_i}(\gamma).
\end{equation}
For $H\leq\Aut(G)$, define the real $H$-orbit of a nontrivial character by
\[
 \mathcal C_H(\gamma)=H\cdot\gamma\ \cup\ H\cdot\gamma^{-1}.
\]
These sets partition $\widehat G\setminus\{1\}$.  They are the orbits obtained
after adjoining character inversion to the $H$-action.  Put
\[
 e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)
   =\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal C}E_{W_i}(\gamma),
 \qquad
 m_{\mathbb R}(H)=\min_{\gamma\neq1}|\mathcal C_H(\gamma)|.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Exact real-orbit identity]\label{cor:real-orbit}
In the experiment of Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-orbit}
 \chitwo\left(P_{R,Z\mid S=s}\middle\Vert P_R\prod_iq_i\right)
 =\sum_{\mathcal C}
   \frac{\prod_i e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)}
        {|\mathcal C|^{d-1}},
\end{equation}
where the sum is over nontrivial real $H$-orbits.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A real orbit $\mathcal C$ is either one inversion-stable $H$-orbit or the
disjoint union of two $H$-orbits $\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal O^{-1}$ of equal
size.  The first case contributes the displayed term directly in
Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit}.  In the second case,
Equation~\eqref{eq:conjugate-energy} gives
$e_i(\mathcal O)=e_i(\mathcal O^{-1})=
e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)/2$ and
$|\mathcal O|=|\mathcal C|/2$.  The sum of the two terms is therefore
\[
 2\frac{\prod_i(e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)/2)}
          {( |\mathcal C|/2)^{d-1}}
 =\frac{\prod_i e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)}{|\mathcal C|^{d-1}}.
\]
\end{proof}

For an integer $k\geq2$, define the packet capacity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-capacity}
 \kappa(k)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&k=2,\\
  \bigl(2\cos^2(\pi/k)\bigr)^{-1},&k\geq3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact conjugate-packet capacity]\label{lem:packet-capacity}
Let $\beta$ be a character of order $k\geq2$, and consider finite-output
channels whose nonconstant spectral energy is supported on
$P_\beta=\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\}$.  The largest possible total energy is
$\kappa(k)$.  Every energy in $[0,\kappa(k)]$ is attainable, split equally
between the characters when $k\geq3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $k=2$, a real centered likelihood response supported on $P_\beta$ has the
form $g(x,z)=a_z\beta(x)$.  Nonnegativity of $1+g$ gives $|a_z|\leq1$, so
$e(W)=\E_z a_z^2\leq1$.  The binary channel
$W(z\mid x)=(1+z\beta(x))/2$ attains one, and scaling its response attains all
smaller energies.

Now let $k\geq3$.  For every output $z$, Fourier inversion and reality give
\[
 g(x,z)=a_z\beta(x)+\overline{a_z}\beta(x)^{-1}
       =2\operatorname{Re}(a_z\beta(x)).
\]
Write $a_z=\rho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}$.  Among the $k$ values of $\beta(x)$,
one lies within angle $\pi/k$ of the direction opposite to
$e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}$.  Hence
\[
 \min_x\operatorname{Re}(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\beta(x))
 \leq-\cos(\pi/k).
\]
Nonnegativity of $1+g(x,z)$ implies
$\rho\leq(2\cos(\pi/k))^{-1}$.  Since the two spectral energies are equal,
\[
 e(W)=2\E_z|a_z|^2\leq\frac{1}{2\cos^2(\pi/k)}.
\]

For attainment, let $\zeta=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/k}$ and choose a phase $\theta$
for which the preceding minimum equals $-\cos(\pi/k)$.  Set
$a_j=(2\cos(\pi/k))^{-1}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\zeta^j$ and
\[
 W(j\mid x)=\frac1k\left(1+2\operatorname{Re}(a_j\beta(x))\right),
 \qquad 0\leq j<k.
\]
Each probability is nonnegative, and they sum to one because
$\sum_j a_j=0$.  The output is uniform under uniform $x$, all $|a_j|$ attain
the radius bound, and the energy is $\kappa(k)$.  Scaling every $a_j$ by a
factor in $[0,1]$ attains every smaller energy.
\end{proof}

Define the usable capacity of the smallest real orbits by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:min-capacity}
 \kappa_{\min}(H)=
 \max_{\substack{\gamma\neq1\\
       |\mathcal C_H(\gamma)|=m_{\mathbb R}(H)}}
       \kappa(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma)).
\end{equation}
Automorphisms and inversion preserve character order, so this quantity is
constant within each orbit considered in the maximum.  Notice that
$\kappa_{\min}(H)>1/2$ for every fixed finite group, although $1/2$ is the
uniform lower bound as character orders grow.

Keep $B$ uniform and independent, but now allow $(h_2,\ldots,h_d)$ to have an
arbitrary joint distribution $D$ on $H^{d-1}$.  Define
\[
 \mathcal R_H(D;\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_d)
 =\sup_{W_i:e(W_i)\leq\varepsilon_i}
   \chitwo(P_{R,Z\mid S=s}\Vert P_R\prod_iq_i).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Real-orbit minimax identity]\label{thm:minimax}
Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite abelian group, $H\leq\Aut(G)$, and
$0\leq\varepsilon_i\leq\kappa_{\min}(H)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
 \inf_{D\text{ on }H^{d-1}}
 \mathcal R_H(D;\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_d)
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{m_{\mathbb R}(H)^{d-1}}.
\end{equation}
Independent uniform automorphisms attain the infimum.  The matching lower bound
uses channels supported only on the conjugate packets of a smallest real orbit
attaining Equation~\eqref{eq:min-capacity}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For independent uniform automorphisms, Corollary~\ref{cor:real-orbit} and
nonnegativity give
\begin{align*}
 \chitwo(P_{R,Z\mid S=s}\Vert P_R\textstyle\prod_iq_i)
 &\leq\frac{1}{m_{\mathbb R}(H)^{d-1}}
      \sum_{\mathcal C}\prod_i e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)\\
 &\leq\frac{1}{m_{\mathbb R}(H)^{d-1}}
      \prod_i\sum_{\mathcal C}e_i^{\mathbb R}(\mathcal C)\\
 &\leq\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{m_{\mathbb R}(H)^{d-1}}.
\end{align*}
The middle inequality retains the diagonal real-orbit products from the
nonnegative product-of-sums expansion.

For the converse, fix a smallest real orbit $\mathcal C$ attaining
Equation~\eqref{eq:min-capacity} and a character
$\beta_1\in\mathcal C$.  Write
\[
 P_\beta=\{\beta,\beta^{-1}\},\qquad r=|P_{\beta_1}|\in\{1,2\}.
\]
The $H$-orbit of $P_{\beta_1}$ consists of $N=|\mathcal C|/r$ character
packets.  For $(h_2,\ldots,h_d)\leftarrow D$, the tuple
\[
 Y=(h_2\cdot P_{\beta_1},\ldots,h_d\cdot P_{\beta_1})
\]
has at most $N^{d-1}$ values.  Choose a value
$y=(P_{\beta_2},\ldots,P_{\beta_d})$ of mass at least $N^{-(d-1)}$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:packet-capacity}, for every $i$ there is a channel with total
energy $\varepsilon_i$, divided equally among the $r$ characters in
$P_{\beta_i}$ and zero elsewhere.  The construction depends only on the packet,
not on the representative.

For arbitrary $D$, the translation average and local-output independence give
the exact diagonal expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arbitrary-D}
 \sum_{\gamma\neq1} E_{W_1}(\gamma)
   \E_{(h_2,\ldots,h_d)\leftarrow D}
      \prod_{i=2}^{d}E_{W_i}(h_i\cdot\gamma).
\end{equation}
No independence among the $h_i$ is used.  For the packet channels above, the
$r$ characters in $P_{\beta_1}$ contribute exactly when $Y=y$.  Hence their
divergence is
\[
 \frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{r^{d-1}}\Pr[Y=y]
 \geq\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(rN)^{d-1}}
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{|\mathcal C|^{d-1}}.
\]
This holds for every $D$ and matches the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Single-real-orbit minimax for all energies]
\label{cor:all-energy}
Let $|G|=Q$ and suppose $H$, together with character inversion, is transitive
on $\widehat G\setminus\{1\}$.  For every feasible budget
$0\leq\varepsilon_i\leq Q-1$,
\[
 \inf_{D\text{ on }H^{d-1}}
 \mathcal R_H(D;\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_d)
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}.
\]
Independent uniform automorphisms attain the infimum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There is one nontrivial real orbit, so Corollary~\ref{cor:real-orbit} gives the
upper bound for independent uniform automorphisms.  Against arbitrary $D$, give
party $i$ the channel from Lemma~\ref{lem:erasure}.  Substitution in
Equation~\eqref{eq:arbitrary-D} gives
\[
 (Q-1)\prod_i\frac{\varepsilon_i}{Q-1}
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}},
\]
independently of $D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal actions on vector spaces]\label{cor:actions}
Let $G\cong\F_p^n$, $Q=p^n$, and suppose
$0<\varepsilon_i\leq\kappa(p)$.  The best minimax value among
automorphism subgroups is
\[
 \frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}.
\]
It is attained exactly when $H$, together with character inversion, acts
transitively on the nontrivial characters.  Equivalently, $H$ is transitive on
the conjugate-character packets.  If $r=1$ for $p=2$ and $r=2$ for odd $p$,
every such $H$ has order at least $(Q-1)/r$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There are $Q-1$ nontrivial characters, so
$m_{\mathbb R}(H)\leq Q-1$, with equality exactly under the stated
transitivity condition.  Such an action exists because
$\operatorname{GL}(n,p)$ is transitive on nonzero dual vectors.  The induced
$H$-action on the $(Q-1)/r$ packets is transitive, so orbit--stabilizer gives
the order bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Field multipliers and projective seed entropy]
\label{cor:entropy}
Let $G$ be the additive group of $F=\F_Q$ and let $H=F^*$ act by scalar
multiplication.  Put $r=1$ in characteristic two and $r=2$ otherwise, and let
$N=(Q-1)/r$.  For every $0\leq\varepsilon_i\leq Q-1$,
\[
 \inf_D\mathcal R_H(D;\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_d)
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}.
\]
Every $D$ whose projection onto $(F^*/\{\pm1\})^{d-1}$ is uniform attains this
value for all feasible budgets.  If
$0<\varepsilon_i<Q-1$ for every $i$, the converse also holds: $D$ attains if
and only if that projection is uniform.  Any seed producing an attaining tuple
deterministically in this strict-interior range therefore has min-entropy at
least $(d-1)\log_2 N$.  In characteristic two, the multiplier tuple itself is
the unique minimizer throughout the strict interior.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Nonzero field scalars act transitively on the nontrivial additive characters,
so their single real orbit has size $Q-1$.  The minimax value for all feasible
budgets follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:all-energy}.

Identify the conjugate-character packets with the abelian group
$K=F^*/\{\pm1\}$, which has order $N$.  For a channel $W_i$, let
$v_i(c)$ be its total spectral energy in packet $c\in K$.  Reality divides that
energy equally among the $r$ characters in the packet.  If
$Y_i\in K$ is the inverse of the projected multiplier and $\mu$ is the joint
law of $(Y_2,\ldots,Y_d)$, Equation~\eqref{eq:arbitrary-D} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-correlation}
 \frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\sum_{c\in K}v_1(c)
   \E_{Y\leftarrow\mu}\prod_{i=2}^d v_i(Y_i c).
\end{equation}
In particular, a uniform $\mu$ makes the $d-1$ aligned packets independent and
uniform.  Equation~\eqref{eq:packet-correlation} is then
$\prod_i e(W_i)/(Q-1)^{d-1}$ for arbitrary channels, proving sufficiency at
every budget.

We prove necessity when every budget is strictly between zero and $Q-1$.  Put
$p=\operatorname{char}(F)$ and $\kappa=\kappa(p)$.  For each
$0<\varepsilon<Q-1$, construct a channel whose packet-energy vector has the
form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spiked-packet-vector}
 v(c)=a+b\mathbf1[c=c_0],\qquad a\geq0,\quad b>0,
\end{equation}
and sums to $\varepsilon$.  If $\varepsilon\leq\kappa$, tagged time sharing
between a capacity-achieving packet channel and an input-independent channel
gives $a=0$ and $b=\varepsilon$.  If
$\kappa<\varepsilon<Q-1$, necessarily $\kappa<Q-1$; tagged time sharing
between the full-reveal endpoint of Lemma~\ref{lem:erasure} and a
capacity-achieving packet channel, with weights
\[
 t=\frac{\varepsilon-\kappa}{Q-1-\kappa},\qquad
 u=\frac{Q-1-\varepsilon}{Q-1-\kappa},
\]
gives $a=rt$ and $b=u\kappa$.  Disjoint output tags make spectral energies the
corresponding convex combination.  Both weights are nonnegative and $b>0$.

Normalize $v_i$ to a probability distribution $p_i=v_i/\varepsilon_i$ on
$K$.  Every Fourier coefficient of $p_i$ is nonzero: the trivial coefficient
is one, while every nontrivial coefficient comes only from the positive spike
in Equation~\eqref{eq:spiked-packet-vector}.  Let independent
$C_i\leftarrow p_i$ and put
\[
 V=(C_2C_1^{-1},\ldots,C_dC_1^{-1})\in K^{d-1}.
\]
For every character tuple $(\eta_2,\ldots,\eta_d)$ of $K^{d-1}$,
\[
 \widehat P_V(\eta_2,\ldots,\eta_d)
 =\widehat p_1\left((\textstyle\prod_{i=2}^d\eta_i)^{-1}\right)
    \prod_{i=2}^d\widehat p_i(\eta_i)\neq0.
\]
Thus convolution by $P_V$ is injective on functions on $K^{d-1}$.
Moving the spike $c_0$ independently in each local channel translates the law
of $V$ by an arbitrary element of $K^{d-1}$.  After normalization,
Equation~\eqref{eq:packet-correlation} is the inner product of $\mu$ with one
of these translates, multiplied by
$r^{1-d}\prod_i\varepsilon_i$.  If $\mu$ is nonuniform, injectivity makes
these inner products nonconstant.  Their average over all translates is
$N^{-(d-1)}$, so one is strictly larger.  The corresponding channels give
divergence strictly larger than
\[
 r^{1-d}\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{N^{d-1}}
 =\frac{\prod_i\varepsilon_i}{(Q-1)^{d-1}}.
\]
Hence a minimizer in the strict interior must have uniform projection.

Finally, if that uniform projected tuple is a deterministic function of a seed
$K_0$, every seed atom lies in an output fiber of probability $N^{-(d-1)}$.
Thus $\max_k\Pr[K_0=k]\leq N^{-(d-1)}$, proving the entropy claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Field-compiler resource accounting]\label{cor:resources}
Fix a section $\sigma:F^*/\{\pm1\}\to F^*$.  Sample $B$ uniformly in $F$ and
sample $(C_2,\ldots,C_d)$ uniformly in
$(F^*/\{\pm1\})^{d-1}$, independently of $B$, then use
$A_i=\sigma(C_i)$.  This realizes the minimax value of
Corollary~\ref{cor:entropy} at every feasible budget using exactly
\[
 \log_2 Q+(d-1)\log_2\frac{Q-1}{r}
\]
bits of random entropy beyond the randomness of the original additive sharing.
The capsule has $Q((Q-1)/r)^{d-1}$ possible values and hence admits a
fixed-length index of
\[
 \left\lceil\log_2 Q+(d-1)\log_2\frac{Q-1}{r}\right\rceil
\]
bits.  In the strict-interior regime, no deterministic multiplier seed attaining
the minimax value can have smaller min-entropy.  The encoded shares remain $d$
field elements.  Given precomputed $A_i^{-1}$, reconstruction uses one field
subtraction, $d-1$ field multiplications, and $d-1$ field additions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The projected multiplier tuple is uniform, so Corollary~\ref{cor:entropy} gives
the minimax value.  Its support has $N^{d-1}$ equally likely elements and is
independent of the $Q$-ary uniform translation, proving the entropy and indexing
claims.  The strict-interior lower bound is the final statement of
Corollary~\ref{cor:entropy}.  The operation count follows directly from
Equation~\eqref{eq:reconstruct}.
\end{proof}

This accounting is for the stated local-automorphism compiler.  It does not
lower-bound the translation randomness of a different compiler or the cost of
arbitrary nonlocal encodings.  Serialized field elements may also require
padding beyond the ideal index lengths above.

\section{Why disclosure order is essential}

\begin{proposition}[Cancellation of metadata-aware bijections]\label{prop:cancellation}
Suppose a local randomized encoding has the form $x=\pi_\rho(u)$, where
$\pi_\rho$ is a bijection and the metadata $\rho$ is available to a leakage
function.  For every leakage function $f$ on the original share, there is a
metadata-aware function
\[
  \ell(\rho,x)=f(\pi_\rho^{-1}(x))
\]
with exactly the same output.  Consequently, arbitrary metadata-aware local
leakage obtains no information-theoretic protection from such bijections.
\end{proposition}

For the field construction, the attack can be made explicit.  If the metadata is
available during leakage, put $p=\operatorname{char}(F)$, let
$\operatorname{Tr}:F\to\F_p$ be the field trace, and set
\[
 \ell_1(B,X_1)=\operatorname{Tr}(X_1-B),\qquad
 \ell_i(A_i,X_i)=\operatorname{Tr}(A_i^{-1}X_i).
\]
The sum of the leaked $p$-ary symbols is $\operatorname{Tr}(s)$.  Thus public
multipliers do not repair the standard character attack.

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}\label{sec:related}

Benhamouda, Degwekar, Ishai, and Rabin initiated the direct Fourier-analytic
study of local leakage for additive and Shamir secret sharing~\cite{bdir18}.
Subsequent work constructed locally leakage-resilient linear schemes and obtained
tight or broader estimates for additive and linear sharing
~\cite{mpsw21,maji22tight,maji22bounded}.  These works establish the basic
dual-code product mechanism on which our calculation depends.

Benhamouda et al. also prove impossibility results for noninteractive local share
conversion from prime-field additive sharing to binary sharing~\cite{bdir18}.
Our transformation is not such a conversion: it preserves the share alphabet,
uses correlated recovery metadata outside the leaking components, and reconstructs
only after that metadata is supplied.  This distinction avoids a formal
contradiction, but it also emphasizes that the capsule is additional protected
state rather than a free local recoding of the original scheme.

Faust, Masure, Micheli, Orlt, and Standaert connect the same Fourier framework to
physical prime-field masking, including exact expressions for average and
worst-case statistical metrics~\cite{faust24practice}.  Their results make clear
that conjugate Fourier harmonics have equal magnitude for real leakage, and use
that symmetry to sharpen a physical-leakage estimate.  We do not claim this
conjugate-energy observation.  Their results also make clear
that the hidden translation in our construction, viewed alone, is not a
substantial mathematical advance: it changes which average is represented in
the transcript.  The candidate contribution here is the channel-independent
$\chitwo$ factorization after automorphism averaging, its orbit decomposition,
and the matching minimax attack.

Jahandideh, Mennink, and Batina analyze ordinary additive masking with
probabilistic noisy leakage, including binary-projection decompositions, tight
success-rate bounds in characteristic two, prime-field estimates, and a security
criterion for finite abelian groups~\cite{jahandideh25decomposition}.  They also
use an erasure/random-probing channel as a comparison object.  Arbitrary noisy
share channels, group-valued masking, projection decompositions, and erasure
witnesses are therefore established ingredients.  Their sharing is not freshly
conjugated by a hidden automorphism action and their objective does not optimize
a distribution on such actions, but this distinction confines our candidate
contribution to the exact capsule-conditioned identity and orbit minimax problem.

Koga and Abe give tighter bounds for the ordinary additive $(n,n)$ scheme over
prime fields by diagonalizing circulant matrices~\cite{koga25circulant}.  Their
stated model maximizes pairwise total variation over deterministic $L$-ary local
leakage functions.  For $L=2$ they determine the asymptotic order and
characterize extremizing leakage functions; for larger $L$ they use
majorization.  This is the closest current antecedent for local spectral
extremizers and may subsume elementary parts of our packet-capacity argument.
It does not make the hidden-randomizer problem unnecessary: our optimization is
over distributions of per-sharing automorphisms, uses stochastic channels and
$\chitwo$ divergence, and is governed by dual-action orbits.  Nevertheless, the
two extremal analyses require a proof-level comparison before any novelty claim
about Lemma~\ref{lem:packet-capacity} is made.  A subsequent Japanese-language
SITA paper by Abe and Koga advertises a general formula for worst-case distance
between one-bit leakages~\cite{abe25general}; its full text is likewise required
for that comparison.

Recent work on Shamir and code-based sharing further narrows the surrounding
claim.  Gupta and Mahdavifar model independent arbitrary noisy leakage channels
on Shamir shares and bound mutual-information, semantic, and distinguishing
leakage~\cite{gupta24statistical}.  Thus neither stochastic local channels nor an
information-theoretic wiretap formulation is new here.  Their objective is to
bound Shamir leakage for a fixed sharing scheme; it does not optimize a hidden
per-sharing automorphism distribution or state a dual-orbit identity.  Klein and
Komargodski sharpen bounds for Shamir sharing over prime fields~\cite{klein23};
Maji, Nguyen, Paskin-Cherniavsky, and Ye construct secure choices over
composite-order fields~\cite{maji24composite}; and Hwang, Maji, Nguyen, and Ye
classify secure evaluation places for physical-bit leakage in specified
regimes~\cite{hwang25places}.  Nguyen proves a physical-bit dichotomy and a
dual-code characterization for linear code-based sharing over binary extension
fields, and uses randomized GRS multipliers to select secure scheme
parameters~\cite{nguyen25linear}.  Random multipliers in secret-sharing design
are therefore prior art.  Those multipliers are sampled to choose a code, rather
than freshly hidden for each sharing and disclosed after a snapshot, but this
distinction concerns the theorem being optimized and not the novelty of scalar
randomization itself.

Adaptive leakage-and-reveal secret sharing allows leakage on selected shares
followed by full revelation of different shares~\cite{ckos21}.  A later
manuscript challenges the published construction's security proof~\cite{hzw22};
we use the former for model comparison, not as a correctness assumption.
Equivocal secret sharing gives stronger simulation behavior under adaptive
probing and explicitly motivates leakage protection for data at rest
~\cite{hvw25}.  Neither inspected source states the recovery-capsule experiment
or exact orbit/minimax identity here.

The standard nonadaptive local-leakage definition lets an adversary choose an
unauthorized set, receive those shares in full, and obtain independently chosen
local leakage from the remaining shares~\cite{sv19}.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:embedding}, our experiment is a designated-set special case
of that established game after the capsule is treated as an oversized share.
Srinivasan and Vasudevan give a general compiler with far stronger rate and
access-structure guarantees.  We therefore make no generic-compiler or new-model
claim; the possible contribution is the exact algebraic characterization of this
simple structured instance.

Principal inertia components give a general spectral description of dependence
through conditional-expectation operators~\cite{calmon17}.  This subsumes the
linear-algebraic interpretation of Lemma~\ref{lem:alphabet}.  Channel comparison
under symmetry and $\chitwo$ divergence is also established in information
theory~\cite{makur16}.  A complete novelty review must therefore cover both
cryptographic leakage and classical channel-symmetrization literature.

\section{Limitations and open directions}

The present result has several sharp limitations.
\begin{itemize}
    \item It is one-shot.  Leakage after capsule disclosure can invert the local
          transformations.
    \item It assumes conditionally independent local channels.  Joint leakage and
          adaptively selected channels are outside the exact identity.
    \item The capsule must occupy a different leakage domain during the snapshot.
    \item Viewed as a share, the capsule is oversized, and the theorem handles only
          the designated unauthorized set containing that share.  It is not a
          full leakage-resilient secret-sharing construction.
    \item The minimax lower bound covers the explicit packet-capacity range in
          Equation~\eqref{eq:min-capacity}.  Larger budgets can force energy onto
          additional characters and require a different extremal analysis when
          there is more than one real orbit.
    \item For field multipliers, projective-uniform randomizers are characterized
          at every strictly interior budget.  Boundary budgets containing zero
          or full-reveal energy can have additional minimizers and are not
          classified here.
    \item Minimax optimality holds within the local-automorphism compiler class,
          not among all leakage-resilient encodings.
    \item The result has not yet been placed in a composable security framework or
          validated against a concrete physical leakage experiment.
\end{itemize}

Natural mathematical questions include characterizing the minimax value beyond
the packet-capacity range and determining the complete set of
minimizing correlated randomizers for general automorphism actions.  The main
cryptographic question is whether the recovery-capsule chronology yields a useful
primitive under a recognized snapshot-leakage model.

\section{Conclusion}

Hidden local automorphisms can turn spectral alignment into an exactly
quantifiable resource when leakage occurs once and the recovery metadata is
disclosed afterward.  Translation orthogonality removes cross-character terms,
while automorphism averaging exposes the dual-orbit energy profile.  In the
single-orbit field case this yields a product identity.  Over arbitrary finite
abelian groups, real single-packet attacks show that the smallest
inversion-closed dual orbit exactly controls the stated minimax problem in the
proved packet range, while full-spectrum erasure channels determine the value at
all energies for single-real-orbit actions.  Field multipliers instantiate the
optimal projectively transitive case.  The same bijections provide no protection when
their metadata is available to the leakage functions, so any application must
treat timing and isolation as part of the security boundary.
